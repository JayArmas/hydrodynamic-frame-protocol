 418 \abstract{We establish the existence of families of linearly stable and causal Bemfica-Disconzi-Noronha-Kovtun
 419 (BDNK) frames for arbitrary equation of state and set of physical transport coefficients. By developing a method 
 420 based on simplification of Routh-Hurwitz criteria and telegrapher factorisation of polynomials, we provide six
 421 concrete examples of such families, three of which admit conformal limits, for both neutral and charged fluids. These frames have adjustable characteristic speeds and can serve 
 422 as a starting point for numerical implementations of relativistic hydrodynamics in various settings.
 423 In the process we uncover a map between stable and causal frames in neutral fluids and stable and causal frames 
 424 in charged fluids. We also develop an \href{https://jacomearmas.org/research/BDNK}{AI research protocol} that can be used to search for such frames in any hydrodynamic theory.

 430 \section{Overview}\label{sec:overview}
 431 
 432 Relativistic hydrodynamics describes the long-wavelength evolution of
 433 conserved energy, momentum and charge. Its applications in nuclear physics
 434 and astrophysics require a consistent treatment of dissipation. However,
 435 constitutive relations truncated at first order in gradients need not
 436 define stable and causal evolution equations when treated as an exact
 437 system~\cite{HiscockLindblom}. This matters in numerical simulations, which also probe
 438 wavelengths outside the hydrodynamic regime: an instability there can
 439 prevent the extraction of long-wavelength observables.
 440 
 441 M\"uller-Israel-Stewart (MIS) theories address this problem by promoting
 442 dissipative stresses and currents to additional variables with relaxation
 443 equations~\cite{Muller,IsraelStewart}. Another approach is the density frame
 444 formulation~\cite{ArmasJain}, in which conserved densities define the
 445 hydrodynamic variables on a chosen time foliation. Its linearized theory
 446 is stable, and boost symmetry is recovered to the retained order in the
 447 derivative expansion. This diffusive formulation provides an alternative
 448 organization of relativistic hydrodynamics, without requiring the
 449 truncated equations to be an exactly causal hyperbolic system.
 450 
 451 The Bemfica-Disconzi-Noronha-Kovtun (BDNK) formulation instead uses
 452 hydrodynamic frame freedom to obtain causal and stable evolution while
 453 retaining covariant constitutive relations in temperature, chemical
 454 potential and velocity~\cite{BDN2018,Kovtun2019,BDN2019,HoultKovtun,BDN2022}.
 455 Time derivatives in the first-order constitutive relations produce
 456 second-order conservation equations and additional nonhydrodynamic modes.
 457 Suitable frame choices give causal, linearly stable systems, with local well-posedness established
 458 for several constitutive classes.
 459 
 460 Numerical studies provide encouraging tests of this formulation. Stable,
 461 convergent evolutions of smooth plane-symmetric conformal flows have been
 462 compared with MIS~\cite{PandyaPretorius}, and conservative finite-volume
 463 methods have been developed for multidimensional flows~\cite{PandyaMostPretorius}.
 464 Nonconformal ideal-gas calculations include heat flow and shock
 465 profiles~\cite{PandyaIdealGas}, while holographically initialized
 466 evolutions test hydrodynamics against microscopic
 467 relaxation~\cite{BantilanBeaFigueras}. Spherically symmetric neutron-star
 468 calculations in the Cowling approximation also exhibit stable evolution
 469 within a restricted parameter range~\cite{ShumEtAl}. These developments motivate further tests of BDNK as an effective
 470 classical description.
 471 
 472 The main obstacle to BDNK hydrodynamics is finding linearly stable and causal
 473 frames for a given equation of state and \emph{physical} transport coefficients.\footnote{By \emph{physical} transport coefficients we mean the coefficients that appear in entropy production and Kubo formulae. In the cases studied here these are bulk and shear viscosities, as well as the conductivity.} General conditions for
 474 causality and stability are available, including charged constructions
 475 \cite{HoultKovtun,BDN2022}, necessary and sufficient conditions for generic
 476 charged frames, including non-thermodynamic choices~\cite{AbboudSperanzaNoronha},\footnote{Non-thermodynamic frames were also considered recently in \cite{Armas:2025uyv, Armas:2026mul} in the context of ultrarelativistic limits.} and a strongly hyperbolic class
 477 with a first-order corrected charge current, constraints on the equation of state, and large frame parameters~\cite{SchianchiAbalos}.
 478 General criteria characterize admissible coefficients, but using them still
 479 requires solving complicated inequalities for a given equation of state and set of
 480 physical transport coefficients. 
 481 
 482 In this work we provide a concrete strategy for finding such frames for any equation of state (EoS) and 
 483 physical transport coefficients. We take the point of view that, within BDNK theory, the EoS and physical transport coefficients are
 484 the properties that characterise the fluid microscopically and can be obtained from e.g. kinetic theory or holography.
 485 The remaining BDNK coefficients can be freely
 486 chosen~\cite{HoultKovtunMicroscopic}. With this in mind, we brute-force
 487 the reduction of the Routh-Hurwitz stability criteria in order to find
 488 analytically tractable solutions and brute-force the factorisation of
 489 the longitudinal (sound) polynomial to simplify the analysis and find
 490 concrete families of frames that are general enough to accommodate
 491 all EoS and physical transport coefficients.
 492 
 493 In Section~\ref{sec:constitutive} we provide the constitutive relations and basic thermodynamic properties of the fluids considered here. In Section~\ref{sec:construction-strategy}
 494 we explain the construction method, allowing both thermodynamic and non-thermodynamic frames.  In sections~\ref{sec:factorization} and
 495 \ref{sec:catalogue} we provide six families of frames and their properties for both neutral and charged fluids while in Section~\ref{sec:discussion} we conclude with a summary and open questions. In
 496 Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} we give various derivations, checks, and proofs.
 497 
 498 \subsection{Constitutive relations and physical data}\label{sec:constitutive}
 499 To begin with, we consider a parity-even fluid with one conserved $U(1)$
 500 current and no external electromagnetic field following mostly the conventions of \cite{HoultKovtun}.
 501 We introduce the timelike fluid velocity $u^\mu$ normalised such that $u^2=-1$, and define the operators $\Delta^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}+u^\mu u^\nu$,
 502 $D=u^\mu\nabla_\mu$, $\Theta=\nabla_\mu u^\mu$,
 503 $a^\mu=Du^\mu$, $\perpgrad^\mu=\Delta^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu$, and
 504 $\alpha=\mu/T$ as well as the shear tensor
 505 \begin{equation}
 506  \sigma^{\mu\nu}=2\Delta^{\mu\rho}\Delta^{\nu\lambda}
 507  \nabla_{(\rho}u_{\lambda)}-\frac23\Delta^{\mu\nu}\Theta .
 508 \end{equation}
 509 The conservation equations determining the evolution of the fluid variables are $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ and
 510 $\nabla_\mu J^\mu=0$, where the most general stress tensor and charge current under the above assumptions are, respectively
 511 \begin{align}
 512  T^{\mu\nu}&=(\epsilon+\mathcal E)u^\mu u^\nu
 513  +(p+\mathcal P)\Delta^{\mu\nu}
 514  +2u^{(\mu}\mathcal Q^{\nu)}-\eta\sigma^{\mu\nu},\label{eq:stress}\\
 515  J^\mu&=(n+\mathcal N)u^\mu+\mathcal J^\mu,\label{eq:current}\\
 516  \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal E\\\mathcal P\\\mathcal N\end{pmatrix}
 517  &=\begin{pmatrix}\eps_1&\eps_2&\eps_3\\
 518  \pi_1&\pi_2&\pi_3\\\nu_1&\nu_2&\nu_3\end{pmatrix}
 519  \begin{pmatrix}D\ln T\\\Theta\\D\alpha\end{pmatrix},\label{eq:scalars}\\
 520  \mathcal Q^\mu&=\theta_1a^\mu+\theta_2\perpgrad^\mu\ln T
 521                          +\theta_3\perpgrad^\mu\alpha,\nonumber\\
 522  \mathcal J^\mu&=\gamma_1a^\mu+\gamma_2\perpgrad^\mu\ln T
 523                          +\gamma_3\perpgrad^\mu\alpha.\label{eq:vectors}
 524 \end{align}
 525 All transport coefficients are functions of the temperature $T$ and chemical potential $\mu$. In the case of neutral fluids they are functions of  $T$ only and six non-trivial coefficients, $\epsilon_{1,2},\pi_{1,2},\theta_{1,2}$, remain while the others vanish. In contrast to the thermodynamic-frame choices used in
 526 Refs.~\cite{BDN2018,Kovtun2019,BDN2019,HoultKovtun,BDN2022},
 527 we do not impose $\theta_1=\theta_2$ or, for charged fluids,
 528 $\gamma_1=\gamma_2$.
 529 We allow these vector coefficients to differ, subject to the entropy
 530 constraint stated below. This enlarges the frame choices without changing
 531 the equilibrium equation of state.
 532 
 533 We are only interested in thermodynamically consistent equations of state (EoS) defined via the pressure $p(T,\mu)$ satisfying $\epsilon=-p+Tp_T+\mu p_\mu$ where we introduced the charge density $n=p_\mu=\partial p/\partial\mu$, the entropy density $s=p_T=\partial p/\partial T$ and the energy density $\epsilon$. To specify the constraints that some of these functions must obey, it is useful to define
 534 \begin{equation}\label{eq:thermo}
 535  r=\frac n w,\qquad A=\left.\frac{\partial p}{\partial\epsilon}\right|_n,
 536  \qquad TB=\left.\frac{\partial p}{\partial n}\right|_\epsilon,
 537  \qquad x=c_s^2=A+rTB~,
 538 \end{equation}
 539 where $w=\epsilon+p$ is the enthalpy and $x$ is the square of the sound speed. We also introduce
 540 \begin{equation}\label{eq:suscept}
 541  \begin{gathered}
 542  E=\left.\partial_T\epsilon\right|_\alpha,\qquad
 543  F=\left.\partial_\alpha\epsilon\right|_T,\qquad
 544  N=\left.\partial_T n\right|_\alpha,\qquad
 545  H=\left.\partial_\alpha n\right|_T,\\[3pt]
 546  \Delta_J=EH-FN=T(\epsilon_Tn_\mu-\epsilon_\mu n_T),\qquad
 547  q_T=N-rE,\qquad q_\alpha=H-rF~,
 548  \end{gathered}
 549 \end{equation}
 550 where the subscript $T$ (or $\mu$) denotes a thermodynamic derivative with respect to $T$ (or $\mu$). Given these functions, we work on a regular and causal thermodynamic domain $T>0$ and $w>0$ with the constraints
 551 \begin{equation}\label{eq:domain}
 552 \begin{split}
 553  &T>0,\quad w>0,\quad\Delta_J>0,\quad 0<x<1,\\
 554  &n_\mu\geq0,\quad E\geq0,\quad
 555  A+\frac{n_\mu}{w}(TB)^2\geq rTB,\quad
 556  n_\mu\geq\frac{n^2}{wx}~.
 557  \end{split}
 558 \end{equation}
 559 The strict inequality in $\Delta_J$ and $x$ avoid singular susceptibilities and degenerate or luminal sound speeds. Five of these conditions follow from equilibrium fluctuation positivity \cite{HoultKovtun} and their derivation is recalled in Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions}. In addition, the second law of thermodynamics enforces \cite{HoultKovtun}
 560 \begin{equation}\label{eq:thermo-domain}
 561 \eta,\zeta,\cD\geq0,\quad  \gamma_2-\gamma_1=r(\theta_2-\theta_1), 
 562 \end{equation}
 563 as we re-derive in Section~\ref{app:sec:charged-uplift}, where $\eta$ is the shear viscosity, $\zeta$ is the bulk viscosity and $\cD$ the conductivity defined as 
 564 \begin{align}
 565  \zeta={}&-\pi_2-\eps_1A^2-T\nu_3B^2+A(\eps_2+\pi_1)
 566        +B(\pi_3+T\nu_2)-AB(\eps_3+T\nu_1),\nonumber\\
 567  \cD T={}&r(\theta_3-rT\theta_1)+rT\gamma_1-\gamma_3.\label{eq:matching}
 568 \end{align}
 569 We consider the EoS $p(T,\mu)$ and the transport coefficients $\eta,\zeta,\cD$ as the physical data characterising the fluid, and derivable for instance via kinetic theory, holography, or any kind of microscopic model. Our goal is to find families of frames, i.e. choices of the remaining coefficients, in terms of this physical data, satisfying \eqref{eq:domain}-\eqref{eq:thermo-domain} but otherwise generic, that render the BDNK theory linearly causal and stable following the strategy described in the next subsection.

 571 \subsection{Frame construction strategy}\label{sec:construction-strategy}
 572 We begin by linearising the conservation equations $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ and
 573 $\nabla_\mu J^\mu=0$ in 3+1 dimensions around a uniform equilibrium state $T\to T+\delta T$, $\mu\to\mu+\delta \mu$ and $u^\mu\to u^\mu+\delta u^\mu$ in flat space $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}$ using plane-wave
 574 perturbations of the form $e^{-i\omega t+i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}$ where $\omega$ is the frequency and $\bold k$ the wavevector.
 575 The frequencies are given by the roots of a dispersion polynomial $P(\varsigma,\mathbf k;\mathbf v)$. Up to a nonzero
 576 normalization, rotational symmetry in the fluid rest frame and boost invariance \cite{Kovtun2019,HoultKovtun} gives
 577 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-full-polynomial}
 578  P(\varsigma,\mathbf k;\mathbf v)
 579  \propto P_T(\varsigma,\mathbf k;\mathbf v)^2P_L(\varsigma,\mathbf k;\mathbf v)=0,
 580  \qquad \varsigma=-i\omega~.
 581 \end{equation}
 582 Here $\mathbf v$ is the equilibrium spatial fluid velocity which can be generated by a boost from the rest state, $\varsigma$ is the growth rate,
 583 $P_T$ is the polynomial factor for each of the two transverse velocity
 584 polarizations, and $P_L$ describes the longitudinal velocity that couples
 585 to temperature and, for charged fluids, to the chemical potential. Our goal is to find dispersion relations $\omega_a(\mathbf k)$ that are linearly stable and causal as we now explain. 
 586 
 587 
 588 \subsubsection{What the roots must satisfy}
 589 For every root $\omega_a$, every real wavevector, and every
 590 uniform velocity $|\mathbf v|<1$, linear spectral stability requires
 591 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-spectral-condition}
 592  \operatorname{Im}\omega_a(\mathbf k;\mathbf v)
 593  =\operatorname{Re}\varsigma_a(\mathbf k;\mathbf v)\leq0.
 594 \end{equation}
 595 This bound must be satisfied for all $\mathbf k$ including $\mathbf k=0$. In order to turn stability into algebraically tractable inequalities, we first work at rest $\mathbf v=0$ and 
 596 define $q=|\mathbf k|^2$. For any real coefficient factor
 597 $p_m(\varsigma,q)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}a_j(q)\varsigma^j$, normalized with $a_m(q)>0$,
 598 the Routh-Hurwitz criterion says that all roots have $\Re \varsigma<0$ if and
 599 only if all its Hurwitz determinants are positive:
 600 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-rh-definition}
 601  \Delta_j(q)=\det\!\left[a_{m+i-2\ell}(q)\right]_{i,\ell=1}^{j}>0,
 602  \qquad j=1,\ldots,m.
 603 \end{equation}
 604 Coefficients outside $0\leq j\leq m$ are set to zero. In particular,
 605 $\Delta_1=a_{m-1}$ and
 606 $\Delta_2=a_{m-1}a_{m-2}-a_ma_{m-3}$. We impose their positivity for every $q>0$ while
 607 zeros at $q=0$ are factored out before testing the remaining roots. At nonzero boosts $\mathbf v\ne 0$ the coefficients generally
 608 become complex, so this real coefficient test is supplemented by the direct boosted proofs in Appendix~\ref{app:arbitrary-boosts}. Causality  in turn additionally requires real,
 609 finite characteristic speeds not exceeding the speed of light. These are the large $\mathbf k$ slopes of the dispersion relations. In particular for $\mathbf k=k\hat{\mathbf k}$,
 610 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-characteristic-condition}
 611  \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\omega_a(k\hat{\mathbf k};\mathbf v)}{k}
 612  =c_a(\hat{\mathbf k};\mathbf v)\in\mathbb R,
 613  \qquad |c_a|\leq1.
 614 \end{equation}
 615 The speed bound is therefore $\lim_{k\to\infty}|\omega_a|/k\leq1$.
 616 We apply it to the principal part of the full local conservation system.
 617 A bound on an isolated dispersion branch is not, by itself, a causality
 618 proof~\cite{HoultKovtunDispersion,GavassinoDisconziNoronha}.
 619 The polynomial criterion also requires that the degree in frequency
 620 match the total derivative degree, so that no additional modes arise
 621 from spatial derivatives after a boost~\cite{HoultKovtunDispersion}.
 622 Below we check this for the full neutral and charged determinants,
 623 and Appendix~\ref{app:arbitrary-boosts} verifies their characteristic
 624 speeds for every propagation angle.

 628 \subsubsection{Transverse sector: the telegrapher equation}\label{sec:strategy-shear}
 629 The rest frame transverse polynomial is the same for neutral and
 630 charged fluids, and given by a simple quadratic polynomial of the form
 631 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-shear-polynomial}
 632  P_T(\varsigma,q)=\theta_1\varsigma^2+w\varsigma+\eta q.
 633 \end{equation}
 634 Indeed, a transverse perturbation has $\delta T=\delta\alpha=0$ and
 635 $\mathbf k\cdot\delta\mathbf u=0$, leaving only the acceleration
 636 coefficient $\theta_1$, enthalpy $w$, and shear viscosity $\eta$ in the linear analysis.
 637 Dividing the polynomial by $w$ gives the Fourier polynomial of the
 638 \emph{telegrapher equation} for a transverse velocity amplitude $V_\perp$:
 639 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-telegrapher}
 640  \tau_T\partial_t^2V_\perp+\partial_tV_\perp-\nu_T\nabla^2V_\perp=0,
 641  \qquad \tau_T=\frac{\theta_1}{w},\quad \nu_T=\frac{\eta}{w}.
 642 \end{equation}
 643 This is the diffusion equation with an additional second time derivative term. The telegrapher form, which we exploit further in the charged case, makes it straightforward to derive  stable and causal bounds on the BDNK coefficients as we now explain. For
 644 $\tau_T,\nu_T>0$ and at small $q$, it has one diffusive root
 645 $\varsigma=-\nu_Tq+O(q^2)$ and one relaxation root tending to $-1/\tau_T$ as $q\to0$.
 646 The quadratic Routh-Hurwitz test is simply positivity of its coefficients while
 647 its squared characteristic speed is $y_T=\nu_T/\tau_T=\eta/\theta_1$.
 648 Consequently the stability and causality requirements \eqref{eq:strategy-rh-definition} and \eqref{eq:strategy-characteristic-condition} reduce to
 649 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-shear-bound}
 650  \theta_1\geq\eta>0,
 651  \qquad \theta_1=\alpha_T\eta,\quad\alpha_T\geq1.
 652 \end{equation}
 653 The choice $\alpha_T>1$ makes this shear channel strictly subluminal. The direct
 654 boosted calculation shows that the same bound controls the shear channel at all
 655 momenta and boosts, including $\alpha_T=1$ (see
 656 Appendix~\ref{app:sec:shear}). We note that it does not require a thermodynamic frame equality between $\theta_1$ and
 657 $\theta_2$. The coefficient $\theta_1$ also enters the longitudinal sector, so its bound must be solved together with the conditions on the longitudinal (sound) channel.
 658 
 659 \subsubsection{Neutral longitudinal sector: reducing the quartic}
 660 In the case of neutral fluids, the longitudinal polynomial is a function of the six frame coefficients
 661 $\varepsilon_{1,2}$, $\pi_{1,2}$ and $\theta_{1,2}$.
 662 A choice of these coefficients will also serve as a \emph{seed} for
 663 the charged construction that follows later. We denote its bulk viscosity invariant
 664 by $\zeta_*$, i.e. for neutral fluids $\zeta_*=\zeta$. We also introduce
 665 $\Gamma=4\eta/3+\zeta_*$, which is the combination controlling sound
 666 attenuation. The second law \eqref{eq:thermo-domain} gives $\Gamma\geq0$,
 667 with $\Gamma>0$ in the positive-shear regime considered here. In this case, the longitudinal polynomial $P_L=P_N$ is given by the quartic
 668 \begin{equation}\label{eq:neutral-sound-polynomial}
 669  P_N=A_L\varsigma^4+B_L\varsigma^3+(w^2+C_Lq)\varsigma^2+D_Lq\varsigma+xw^2q+F_Lq^2~,
 670 \end{equation}
 671 with coefficients in the form
 672 \begin{align}
 673  A_L&=x\varepsilon_1\theta_1, \quad B_L=w(x\varepsilon_1+\theta_1), \quad C_L=x\{\varepsilon_1(4\eta/3-\pi_2)+\varepsilon_2(\theta_2+\pi_1)+\theta_1\pi_1\},\nonumber\\
 674  D_L&=xB_L+w\Gamma,\quad F_L=x\theta_2(\pi_2-4\eta/3),\label{eq:quartic-data}\\
 675 \zeta_*&= -\pi_2-x^2\varepsilon_1+x(\varepsilon_2+\pi_1). \nonumber
 676 \end{align}
 677 The last relation is the neutral specialization of \eqref{eq:matching}.
 678 Before applying Routh-Hurwitz, we check the zero-momentum modes:
 679 \begin{equation}\label{eq:neutral-rest-gaps}
 680  P_N(\varsigma,0)=\varsigma^2(x\varepsilon_1\varsigma+w)
 681                                   (\theta_1\varsigma+w).
 682 \end{equation}
 683 For nonzero $\varepsilon_1,\theta_1$, this gives two gapless modes and the
 684 gaps $-w/(x\varepsilon_1)$ and $-w/\theta_1$. Since $w,x>0$, damping of
 685 both gaps requires $\varepsilon_1,\theta_1>0$.
 686 We work in this nondegenerate regime and introduce the dimensionless combinations
 687 \begin{equation}\label{eq:invariants}
 688  R=\frac{D_L}{B_L}=x+\frac{\Gamma}{x\varepsilon_1+\theta_1},\qquad
 689  \Sigma=\frac{C_L}{A_L},\qquad\Phi=\frac{F_L}{A_L},\qquad
 690  \Lh=R(\Sigma-R)-\Phi~,
 691 \end{equation}
 692 in terms of which the Hurwitz determinants defined in \eqref{eq:strategy-rh-definition} gives
 693 \begin{align}\label{eq:rh}
 694  \Delta_1&=B_L,\qquad
 695  \Delta_4=(xw^2q+F_Lq^2)\Delta_3,\nonumber\\
 696  \Delta_2&=B_Lw^2+A_LB_L(\Sigma-R)q,\nonumber\\
 697  \Delta_3&=B_Lw^3\Gamma q+A_LB_L^2\Lh q^2.
 698 \end{align}
 699 The requirement at every momentum has thus become a sign problem for
 700 the coefficients of these low-degree polynomials in $q$.
 701 For causality, substituting $\varsigma=-ick$, $q=k^2$ into the terms of
 702 total derivative order four gives $y^2-\Sigma y+\Phi=0$, where
 703 $y=c^2$. The squared longitudinal and transverse speeds are
 704 \begin{equation}\label{eq:speeds}
 705  y_\pm=\frac{\Sigma\pm\sqrt{\Sigma^2-4\Phi}}2,
 706  \qquad y_T=\frac{\eta}{\theta_1}.
 707 \end{equation}
 708 A sufficient set of rest frame design inequalities is therefore
 709 \begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 710  \Phi\geq0,\qquad \Lh\geq0,\qquad
 711  0\leq y_-\leq y_+\leq1,\qquad \theta_1\geq\eta.
 712 \end{equation}
 713 Indeed, $\Lh\geq0$ places $R$ between $y_-$ and $y_+$, so
 714 $\Sigma\geq R$ and all four Hurwitz determinants are strictly
 715 positive at every $q>0$. The remaining task is to choose actual
 716 frame coefficients satisfying these inequalities and entropy matching $\zeta_*=\zeta$. In summary, the Routh-Hurwitz inequalities and causality condition for a quartic polynomial are still analytically tractable - a realisation that we will be exploiting in the charged case.

 718 \subsubsection{Charged longitudinal sector: extracting a telegrapher factor}
 719 For charged fluids, $P_L=P_{\rm ch}$ comes from three coupled
 720 longitudinal equations and is generically a sextic:
 721 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-charged-sextic}
 722  P_{\rm ch}(\varsigma,q)=\sum_{j=0}^{6}a_j(q)\varsigma^j.
 723 \end{equation}
 724 Directly imposing its six Routh-Hurwitz inequalities for all $q>0$ is
 725 considerably harder to solve explicitly for arbitrary physical inputs. We instead seek frame coefficients for
 726 which the original determinant factorizes exactly as
 727 \begin{equation}\label{eq:strategy-factorization}
 728  P_{\rm ch}(\varsigma,q)=\mathcal N_0F_q(\varsigma,q)P_N(\varsigma,q),\qquad
 729  F_q(\varsigma,q)=\tau_q\varsigma^2+\varsigma+D_qq.
 730 \end{equation}
 731 Here $\mathcal N_0\ne0$ is an overall normalisation that depends on thermodynamic data, $D_q$ is
 732 a charge diffusivity, and $\tau_q$ is its relaxation time to be defined precisely below. The factor
 733 $F_q$ has precisely the telegrapher form introduced in the shear
 734 sector in \eqref{eq:strategy-shear-polynomial}. With $D_q>0$, choosing $\tau_q\geq D_q$ gives stable rest
 735 roots and a squared characteristic speed $y_q=D_q/\tau_q\leq1$.
 736 The other factor has the neutral quartic form, evaluated at the charged
 737 values $w,x,\zeta_*$, where
 738 $\zeta_*=\zeta+\mathcal D T^2B^2/x$ as defined in \eqref{eq:effective}.
 739 The charged bulk viscosity remains the prescribed $\zeta$ in
 740 \eqref{eq:matching}. Thus the search reduces to realizing this
 741 factorization while preserving the physical transport coefficients. At rest and $q=0$, $P_{\rm ch}$ gives three gapless and three
 742 gapped modes.\footnote{For positive transport and nonzero leading coefficients, the frequency
 743 and total derivative degrees agree: eight for $P_T^2P_N$ and ten for
 744 $P_T^2F_qP_N$, as required by the polynomial causality
 745 criterion~\cite{HoultKovtunDispersion}. Appendix~\ref{app:arbitrary-boosts}
 746 verifies that these degrees are unchanged under finite boosts.
 747 The equality of frequency and total derivative degrees also holds when
 748 certain transport coefficients vanish.}

 750 \subsubsection{Five step frame construction strategy} \label{sec:constructionstrategy}
 751 Below we detail the strategy used to find families of BDNK frames for
 752 any given EoS and transport coefficients.
 753 \begin{enumerate}
 754 \item \textbf{Solve the entropy relations.}
 755 The first step is to solve the entropy relations \eqref{eq:matching} for some of the BDNK coefficients in terms of the physical coefficients $\cD, \zeta$ and the remaining BDNK coefficients.
 756 In the neutral case, for instance, we solve the last relation in
 757 \eqref{eq:quartic-data}, for example as
 758 $\pi_1=x\varepsilon_1+(\zeta_*+\pi_2)/x-\varepsilon_2$. The remaining coefficients will be fixed in the later steps.
 759 
 760 \item \textbf{Choose branches that make the reduced quartic inequalities solvable.}
 761 Next, we compute $R,\Sigma,\Phi,\Lh$ using the coefficients in the previous step and impose
 762 \eqref{eq:certificate}. To simplify the inequalities, we set $\pi_2=4\eta/3$ in order to obtain $\Phi=0$, which reduces the sound bounds to
 763 $R\leq\Sigma\leq1$. Alternatively taking $\varepsilon_2=0$ extracts the exact factor
 764 $\theta_1\varsigma+w$ from the polynomial, leaving a simpler cubic polynomial to analyse. Keeping $\varepsilon_2\ne0$
 765 and choosing $\Sigma=1$ leaves room for families that admit conformal limits. Other simplifying alternatives are possible and we discuss them in Section~\ref{sec:factorization}.
 766 
 767 \item \textbf{Optional: impose a finite conformal limit.}
 768 Certain EoS and transport coefficients may admit a conformal point. At
 769 that point, conformal symmetry requires finite coefficients satisfying
 770 \cite{Kovtun2019,HoultKovtun}
 771 \begin{equation}\label{eq:conformal}
 772  \eps_i=3\pi_i,\quad \pi_1=3\pi_2,\quad \nu_1=3\nu_2,
 773  \quad\theta_1=\theta_2,\quad\gamma_1=\gamma_2.
 774 \end{equation}
 775 In the neutral case, these constraints are equivalently expressed by requiring the seed
 776 to approach 
 777 \[
 778  (\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\pi_1,\pi_2,\theta_1,\theta_2)
 779  \longrightarrow(3\Pi,\Pi,\Pi,\Pi/3,\theta,\theta)
 780 \]
 781 as $x\to1/3$ and $\zeta_*\to0$, with finite $\Pi,\theta$. This means that non-thermodynamic frame choices are allowed away
 782 from the conformal point, but $\theta_2-\theta_1$ must vanish at the conformal point. 
 783 
 784 \item \textbf{Uplift each neutral seed to the charged factorization.}
 785 Each admissible neutral seed can be \emph{uplifted} to a charged family by
 786 choosing the additional coefficients so that, after linearising about
 787 uniform equilibrium and Fourier transforming in its rest frame,
 788 $\delta[\nabla_\mu J^\mu+r u_\nu\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}]
 789 =F_q(\varsigma,q)h=0$.
 790 Here $h=\delta n-r\delta\epsilon=q_T\delta T+q_\alpha\delta\alpha$,
 791 and $r=n/w$ is evaluated at equilibrium. The coefficient map in
 792 Section~\ref{sec:charged-factorization-overview} realizes
 793 \eqref{eq:strategy-factorization}, with
 794 \begin{equation}\label{eq:effective}
 795  \begin{gathered}
 796  \Xi=q_\alpha-rT^2q_T=\frac{T\Delta_Jx}{w}>0,\qquad
 797  D_q=\frac{\cD T}{\Xi},\qquad
 798  \tau_q=\alpha_qD_q,\quad\alpha_q\geq1,\\[3pt]
 799  \zeta_* =\zeta+\frac{\cD T^2B^2}{x}.
 800  \end{gathered}
 801 \end{equation}
 802 Here $\Xi$ is a charge susceptibility and $\alpha_q$ is a free
 803 dimensionless relaxation parameter. Entropy matching \eqref{eq:matching}
 804 requires evaluating the neutral seed at the charged values $w,x,\zeta_*$.
 805 The physical charged bulk viscosity remains the prescribed $\zeta$.
 806 
 807 \item \textbf{Verify the original determinant, boosts and boundaries.}
 808 We substitute the proposed coefficients into the constitutive relations to verify \eqref{eq:matching} with the prescribed transport coefficients and compute the determinant of the resulting linearized conservation equations to verify the factorization of the polynomial exactly. We then use the boosted polynomial
 809 at real momentum, including its actual $\mathbf k=0$
 810 gaps to make sure that the boosted modes are stable.  In practice, in Appendix~\ref{app:arbitrary-boosts} we show that the reduced quartic inequalities and telegrapher bounds already ensure stability and causality in every inertial frame. Hence no additional coefficient inequalities arise from boosting, and the result applies to every family of frames found by following the previous steps above. When frame coefficients or physical transport coefficients vanish, the
 811 polynomials can change degree. We then check the reduced equations
 812 directly, as summarized in footnotes~\ref{fn:neutral-zero-transport},
 813 \ref{fn:shear-zero-transport} and \ref{fn:charged-zero-transport}.
 814 \end{enumerate}
 815 The steps outlined above, together with brute-force polynomial
 816 factorisation, form the basis of the
 817 \href{https://jacomearmas.org/research/BDNK}{AI research protocol}. 
 818 We will now apply this strategy to neutral and charged fluids.

 821 \subsection{Neutral construction and charged telegrapher factorization}\label{sec:factorization}
 822 We apply the strategy of Section~\ref{sec:construction-strategy} to
 823 construct the neutral families \family{1}--\family{3}, \family{4}, \family{5} and \family{6}, and then uplift

 827 \subsubsection{Neutral branches without conformal limits}
 828 \label{sec:neutral-branches-overview}
 829 We begin by solving the reduced conditions \eqref{eq:certificate} by choosing the
 830 remaining frame coefficients after entropy matching \eqref{eq:matching}. Setting $\pi_2=4\eta/3$ makes $\Phi=0$ and reduces the sound
 831 constraints to the interval $R\leq\Sigma\leq1$. Setting $\varepsilon_2=0$ yields, for any $\theta_2$, the exact factorization
 832 \begin{equation}\label{eq:relax-factor}
 833  P_N=(\theta_1\varsigma+w)\bigl[x\varepsilon_1\varsigma^3+w\varsigma^2+x\pi_1q\varsigma+xwq\bigr].
 834 \end{equation}
 835 Equation~\eqref{eq:relax-factor} is an exact simplification of the neutral sound polynomial:
 836 it separates the momentum-independent relaxation root $\varsigma=-w/\theta_1$
 837 (when $\theta_1>0$) from a cubic polynomial describing two sound modes and a second relaxation mode.
 838 Using entropy matching \eqref{eq:matching}, the cubic part of the polynomial becomes
 839 \[
 840  (w+x\varepsilon_1\varsigma)(\varsigma^2+xq)+\Gamma \varsigma q.
 841 \]
 842 The term $\Gamma \varsigma q$ couples sound to relaxation.\footnote{\label{fn:neutral-quadratic-obstruction}One can show that the full
 843 neutral sound quartic polynomial cannot factor exactly into two local scalar quadratic
 844 polynomials in $(\varsigma,k)$, with coefficients depending only on state
 845 and transport data, for a family with a finite, stable and causal conformal
 846 limit and $\eta_c>0$. Matching coefficients in this factorization ansatz,
 847 together with the conformal relations, forces
 848 $\eta_c(2\Pi+\eta_c)=0$, whereas stability requires $\Pi\geq4\eta_c>0$.
 849 This obstruction concerns the stated local quadratic ansatz. It does
 850 not exclude factorizations with non-polynomial momentum dependence.} Its cubic
 851 Routh-Hurwitz inequality reduces to positivity of $\Gamma$ which is already guaranteed by the second law \eqref{eq:thermo-domain} while
 852 causality fixes $\varepsilon_1\geq\Gamma/[x(1-x)]$. Together with $\theta_1\geq\eta$ from \eqref{eq:strategy-shear-bound},
 853 this defines the families \family{1} and \family{2} by choosing $\theta_2=0$ (non-thermodynamic) and $\theta_2=\theta_1$ (thermodynamic), respectively.
 854 The family \family{1} allows equality in both causality bounds, $\varepsilon_1=\Gamma/[x(1-x)]$ and $\theta_1=\eta$.
 855 
 856 Alternatively, we can retain $\varepsilon_2\ne0$ and impose $\Sigma=1$, thereby determining
 857 \begin{equation}\label{eq:luminal-design}
 858  \theta_2=\frac{(\varepsilon_1-\pi_1)\theta_1-\varepsilon_2\pi_1}{\varepsilon_2},\qquad
 859  x\varepsilon_1+\theta_1\geq\frac{\Gamma}{1-x}.
 860 \end{equation}
 861 This defines a general luminal branch: $\varepsilon_1$, $\varepsilon_2\ne0$
 862 and $\theta_1$ remain free, subject to $\varepsilon_1>0$,
 863 $\theta_1\geq\eta$ and the inequality in \eqref{eq:luminal-design}.
 864 Entropy matching fixes $\pi_1=x\varepsilon_1+\Gamma/x-\varepsilon_2$,
 865 and \eqref{eq:luminal-design} fixes $\theta_2$.
 866 The particular choices $\varepsilon_2=-2\eta$ and $\theta_1=\eta$
 867 give rise to the family that we refer to as \family{3} and its remaining bound on $\varepsilon_1$ below.
 868 In summary, the three prescriptions just described are
 869 \begin{align}
 870  \family{1},\family{2}:\quad &(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\pi_1,\pi_2,\theta_1)
 871        =(e,0,xe+\Gamma/x,4\eta/3,\alpha_T\eta),
 872        \quad e=\frac{\lambda\Gamma}{x(1-x)},\nonumber\\
 873  &\theta_2=0\ \text{(\family{1})},\qquad \theta_2=\theta_1\ \text{(\family{2})},
 874        \quad\lambda,\alpha_T\geq1,\nonumber\\
 875  \family{3}:\quad &\varepsilon_2=-2\eta,\quad \pi_1=x\varepsilon_1+\Gamma/x+2\eta,
 876        \quad \pi_2=4\eta/3,\quad \theta_1=\eta,\nonumber\\
 877  &\theta_2=-(\varepsilon_1+\pi_1)/2,\qquad
 878        \varepsilon_1\geq\frac{\Gamma-\eta(1-x)}{x(1-x)}.\label{eq:S-seeds}
 879 \end{align}
 880 These bounds ensure $\varepsilon_1,\theta_1>0$, so the gapped modes
 881 in \eqref{eq:neutral-rest-gaps} are damped. For \family{3}, positivity
 882 follows from $\Gamma-\eta(1-x)=\zeta_*+\eta(x+1/3)>0$.
 883 Here $\lambda$ increases the scalar relaxation coefficient and
 884 $\alpha_T$ increases the shear relaxation time relative to their causal
 885 lower bounds and can be chosen freely. At $\lambda=\alpha_T=1$, the family \family{1} has
 886 $\pi_1=\varepsilon_1$. Substituting its coefficients \eqref{eq:S-seeds}
 887 into the characteristic-speed formulas \eqref{eq:speeds} gives
 888 $y_-=0$ and $y_+=y_T=1$: one longitudinal characteristic speed vanishes,
 889 while the other longitudinal speed and the shear speed equal the speed of light.
 890 For positive limiting shear viscosity, these families do not admit
 891 conformal limits, as shown in Section~\ref{app:sec:neutral-family-derivations}.
 892 We now return to the general branch to construct families with conformal limits.

 894 \subsubsection{Neutral families with finite conformal limits}
 895 \label{sec:conformal-construction}
 896 We now construct the neutral families \family{4}, \family{5} and \family{6} by solving the reduced inequalities and then
 897 imposing the finite conformal limits required by \eqref{eq:conformal}. To construct \family{4}, we return to the general branch \eqref{eq:luminal-design}
 898 with $\pi_2=4\eta/3$ and $\Sigma=1$, before imposing \family{3}'s choices
 899 $\varepsilon_2=-2\eta$ and $\theta_1=\eta$.
 900 A well defined conformal limit requires $\varepsilon_2\to3\pi_2=4\eta_c>0$
 901 when the limiting shear viscosity $\eta_c$ is positive. We therefore choose $\varepsilon_2$ and $\pi_1$ both positive and parametrise their freedom in the following way.
 902 Entropy matching \eqref{eq:matching}  fixes only the sum
 903 $\varepsilon_2+\pi_1=x\varepsilon_1+\Gamma/x$ so we can parametrise the remaining freedom by
 904 $\varepsilon_1=L\Gamma$, $\theta_1=K\Gamma$ for some $L$, and a fraction $f$ of
 905 that sum assigned to $\pi_1$. The luminal condition
 906 \eqref{eq:luminal-design} then fixes $\theta_2$, giving
 907 \begin{align}
 908  \varepsilon_1&=L\Gamma,\quad\varepsilon_2=(1-f)S_0\Gamma,\quad \pi_1=fS_0\Gamma, \quad S_0=xL+1/x,\nonumber\\
 909  \pi_2&=4\eta/3,\theta_1=K\Gamma,\quad  \theta_2=\Gamma\left[\frac{K(L-fS_0)}{(1-f)S_0}-fS_0\right].\label{eq:AG}
 910 \end{align}
 911 The choices
 912 $L\geq2/[x(1-x)]$, $K\geq3/4$ and $0<f<1$ solve the remaining bounds as they
 913  give $R\leq(1+x)/2<1$, $\theta_1\geq\eta$ and a nonzero
 914 denominator in $\theta_2$. Thus $\Phi=0$, $\Sigma=1$ and
 915 $\Lh=R(1-R)>0$ satisfying \eqref{eq:certificate}. These bounds establish stability and causality but not yet a conformal limit. In order to do so we assume that  $\eta_c>0$ denotes the limiting conformal form of the shear viscosity. Since
 916 $\pi_2\to4\eta_c/3$, Step~3 in Section~\ref{sec:constructionstrategy} fixes $\Pi=4\eta_c$.
 917 The limits $\varepsilon_1\to3\Pi$ and
 918 $\varepsilon_2,\pi_1\to\Pi$ therefore require $L\to9$ and $f\to1/2$.
 919 Using these scalar limits and $\theta_1\to\theta$, the luminal
 920 condition \eqref{eq:luminal-design} gives $\theta_2\to2\theta-4\eta_c$. Requiring
 921 $\theta_2\to\theta$ fixes $\theta=4\eta_c$, hence $K\to3$.
 922 These implement the conformal restrictions leading to a family \family{4} with a well defined conformal limit and is in general a non-thermodynamic frame.
 923 
 924 In turn, to construct the family \family{5} with a conformal limit, we first pick the \family{4} scalar parameters to be
 925 $L=2/[x(1-x)]$ and $f=1/2$, which satisfy the preceding bounds and
 926 conformal restrictions and imply
 927 $m=\varepsilon_2=\pi_1$. Contrary to the family \family{4}, we now impose a thermodynamic frame by choosing $\theta_1=\theta_2=\theta_0$ and hence \eqref{eq:luminal-design} gives
 928 $(\varepsilon_1-2m)\theta_0=m^2$. This determines the parameters
 929 \begin{equation}\label{eq:balanced}
 930  \varepsilon_1=\frac{2\Gamma}{x(1-x)},\quad
 931  m=\frac{\Gamma(1+x)}{2x(1-x)},\quad \pi_2=\frac43\eta,
 932  \quad \theta_0=\frac{\Gamma(1+x)^2}{4x(1-x)^2}.
 933 \end{equation}
 934 Keeping these parameters as they are, we can deform away from the luminal branch $\Phi=0,\Sigma=1$ by increasing slightly the coefficients $\theta_1,\theta_2$ such that
 935 $\theta_1=\theta_2=\lambda\theta_0$, with $\lambda>1$. In this case the condition $\Phi=0$ remains unchanged, while
 936 \[
 937  \Sigma=\rho_0=\frac{1+x}{2}+\frac{1-x}{2\lambda}<1.
 938 \]
 939 For these choices one finds that $R<\rho_0$, and
 940 $\lambda\theta_0>\eta$ also makes the shear speed \eqref{eq:speeds} strictly subluminal. It is possible to slightly generalise this family to non-thermodynamic frames by keeping
 941 $\theta_1=\lambda\theta_0$ and choosing
 942 $\theta_2=\theta_1+\chi(\lambda-1)m$, where
 943 \begin{equation}\label{eq:chi}
 944  \lambda>1,\qquad
 945  \chi=\beta\frac{(3x-1)^2}{1+(3x-1)^2},\qquad0\leq\beta\leq1.
 946 \end{equation}
 947 Here the parameter $\beta$ parametrises the freedom in choosing $\chi$. This changes the upper squared sound speed $y_+$ \eqref{eq:speeds} by a fraction $\chi<1$ of
 948 the available margin to the light cone:
 949 \[
 950  y_-=0,\qquad y_+=\rho=\rho_0+\chi(1-\rho_0)
 951  =1-\frac{(1-x)(\lambda-1)(1-\chi)}{2\lambda}<1.
 952 \]
 953 Hence $R<\rho_0\leq\rho<1$ preserves the reduced stability and
 954 causality inequalities. The explicit positive margins are proved in
 955 Section~\ref{app:sec:neutral-conformal-derivations}.
 956 Since $\chi\to0$ at conformality, finite parameters with
 957 $\lambda\to\lambda_c>1$ give the Step~3 limits in Section~\ref{sec:constructionstrategy}
 958 $\Pi=4\eta_c$ and $\theta=4\lambda_c\eta_c$.
 959 Thus \family{5} achieves strict subluminality without losing its conformal limit while
 960 its lower sound speed $y_-$ remains zero.
 961 
 962 The family \family{6} that we now describe allows for non-zero lower sound speed. In order to construct it, 
 963 we choose $\pi_2>4\eta/3$ and $\theta_2>0$,
 964 which makes $\Phi>0$ in \eqref{eq:quartic-data}. We introduce a parameter
 965 $\Lambda$ to parametrise this freedom, while the function $\chi$ in
 966 \eqref{eq:chi} controls the difference between the $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$. Choosing $1<\Lambda\leq2$, $0\leq\beta\leq1$, this leads to
 967 \begin{align}
 968  \varepsilon_1&=\frac{2\Lambda\Gamma}{x(1-x)},&
 969  \varepsilon_2=\pi_1&=\frac{\Lambda\Gamma(1+x)}{2x(1-x)},\nonumber\\
 970  \pi_2&=\frac43\eta+(\Lambda-1)\Gamma,&
 971  \theta_1&=\frac{\Lambda\Gamma(1+x)^2}{2x(1-x)^2},\nonumber\\
 972  \theta_2&=\theta_1\left[1+\frac{4\chi(1-\Sigma_0)}{1+x}\right],&
 973  \Sigma_0&=\frac{3+x}{4}
 974  -\frac{2(\Lambda-1)x(1-x)^2}{\Lambda(1+x)^2}.
 975  \label{eq:AN}
 976 \end{align}
 977 The squared sound speed combinations defined in \eqref{eq:invariants} are
 978 then
 979 \begin{align}
 980  \Sigma&=\Sigma_0+\chi(1-\Sigma_0),&
 981  \Phi&=\frac{(\Lambda-1)x(1-x)}{2\Lambda}
 982        +\frac{2(\Lambda-1)x(1-x)}{\Lambda(1+x)}\chi(1-\Sigma_0),
 983        \nonumber\\
 984  R&=x+\frac{2x(1-x)^2}{\Lambda(1+6x-3x^2)},\label{eq:AN-speeds}
 985 \end{align}
 986 and satisfy $\Phi>0$, $\Sigma<1$ and $\Lh>0$ given in \eqref{eq:certificate}. The resulting neutral speed bounds are
 987 \begin{equation}\label{eq:AN-bounds}
 988  0<y_-<R<y_+<1,\qquad 0<y_T<\frac1{2\Lambda}<1.
 989 \end{equation}
 990 The choice
 991 $\beta=0$ yields a thermodynamic frame while $\beta>0$ permits
 992 non-thermodynamic frames away from $x=1/3$. Bounded parameters with
 993 finite conformal limits yield finite coefficients. In particular,
 994 $\Lambda\to\Lambda_c$ and $\chi\to0$ give the Step~3 limits (see Section~\ref{sec:constructionstrategy})
 995 $\Pi=4\Lambda_c\eta_c$ and $\theta=8\Lambda_c\eta_c$.
 996 For $\eta_c>0$ and $1<\Lambda_c\leq2$, both longitudinal speeds
 997 remain nonzero and strictly subluminal at conformality. The full derivation
 998 is given in Section~\ref{app:sec:neutral-conformal-derivations}.

1000 \subsubsection{Charged uplift: an additional exact telegrapher factor}
1001 \label{sec:charged-factorization-overview}
1002 Having constructed the neutral families \family{1}-\family{3}, \family{4}, \family{5} and \family{6}, we now
1003 provide the coefficient map between neutral and charged frames implementing Step~4 in Section~\ref{sec:constructionstrategy}. Assuming $\cD>0$, we take
1004 $D_q,\tau_q,\zeta_*$ from \eqref{eq:effective} and evaluate the chosen
1005 neutral families at the charged values of $w,x,\eta$, with
1006 $\Gamma=4\eta/3+\zeta_*$. In addition we retain the values of the six stress coefficients
1007 $\varepsilon_{1,2},\pi_{1,2},\theta_{1,2}$ as given by each family and set the charge coefficients according to
1008 \begin{align}
1009  \eps_3&=rT\eps_1,\qquad \pi_3=rT\pi_1,\qquad
1010  \theta_3=rT\theta_2,\label{eq:uplift-stress}\\
1011  (\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3)&=
1012  (r\varepsilon_1+T\tau_qq_T,\ r\varepsilon_2+TD_qq_T,\ r^2T\varepsilon_1+\tau_qq_\alpha),\nonumber\\
1013  (\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3)&=
1014  (r\theta_1-TD_qq_T,\ r\theta_2-TD_qq_T,\ r^2T\theta_2-D_qq_\alpha).
1015  \label{eq:uplift-current}
1016 \end{align}
1017 These coefficients preserve the entropy identities $\eta,\zeta,\cD$ in
1018 \eqref{eq:matching}, factorise the polynomial into a telegrapher part times the quartic
1019 and fix the normalization in \eqref{eq:strategy-factorization} such that
1020 \begin{equation}\label{eq:factorization}
1021  \det M_{\rm charged}=-\frac{\Delta_J}{w}F_q(\varsigma,q)P_N(\varsigma,q).
1022 \end{equation}
1023 We verify this factorization explicitly  in
1024 Appendix~\ref{app:sec:charged-uplift} while stability at arbitrary boosts is verified in Appendix~\ref{app:sec:charged-boost-proof}.  With this map, choosing $\alpha_q>1$ makes
1025 the extra squared speed $y_q=1/\alpha_q$ strictly subluminal as described in
1026 Section~\ref{sec:construction-strategy}. For a regular conformal EoS limit with $\zeta\to0$, one has
1027 $B,q_T\to0$ and $\zeta_*\to0$ leading to finite charged coefficients satisfying the conformal requirements \eqref{eq:conformal}.
1028 
1029 We note that in the case of the \family{6} family, choosing $\alpha_q>1$, the bounds \eqref{eq:AN-bounds}, together with
1030 $0<y_q=1/\alpha_q<1$, make every comoving characteristic speed nonzero
1031 and strictly subluminal when $\eta,\cD>0$. As a useful example, at the conformal point, picking $\Lambda=\alpha_q=2$ gives
1032 \begin{equation}\label{eq:benchmark}
1033  (\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\pi_1,\pi_2,\theta_1,\theta_2)
1034  =\eta(24,8,8,8/3,16,16),\qquad
1035  (y_-,y_+,y_T,y_q)=(1/12,2/3,1/16,1/2),
1036 \end{equation}
1037 as well as $q_T=0$ and $\Xi=q_\alpha$ so
1038 \eqref{eq:effective} gives $D_qq_\alpha=\cD T$ and
1039 $\tau_qq_\alpha=2\cD T$. The current coefficients in
1040 \eqref{eq:uplift-current} therefore become
1041 \begin{align}
1042  (\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3)
1043    &=(24r\eta,\,8r\eta,\,24r^2T\eta+2\cD T),\nonumber\\
1044  (\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3)
1045    &=(16r\eta,\,16r\eta,\,16r^2T\eta-\cD T).
1046  \label{eq:benchmark-current}
1047 \end{align}
1048 Here $r=n/w$ and the physical conductivity $\cD$ remains a free parameter, in particular, $\nu_1=3\nu_2$ and $\gamma_1=\gamma_2$
1049 satisfy the conformal current relations in \eqref{eq:conformal}. We overview all families in the next subsection.

1126 \subsection{Discussion and numerical use}\label{sec:discussion}
1127 Table~\ref{tab:families} provides explicit coefficient prescriptions that
1128 can be used in numerical implementations of neutral and charged BDNK
1129 hydrodynamics. Given an equation of state and prescribed
1130 $\eta,\zeta,\mathcal D$, one evaluates the thermodynamic quantities,
1131 checks \eqref{eq:domain}--\eqref{eq:thermo-domain}, and selects frame
1132 parameters satisfying the bounds of the chosen family. The neutral
1133 coefficients and the common charged uplift are then given in
1134 Sections~\ref{sec:neutral-branches-overview}--\ref{sec:charged-factorization-overview}.
1135 The construction avoids solving the full stability inequalities anew
1136 for each equation of state.
1137 
1138 The families allow different choices of characteristic structure while
1139 keeping physical transport fixed. With positive shear viscosity and
1140 conductivity, \family{6} has strictly positive, strictly subluminal
1141 squared comoving characteristic speeds, including at its finite conformal
1142 endpoint. It is therefore a natural starting point. Families \family{1}-\family{5} instead
1143 allow a zero sound characteristic speed, and some also allow luminal
1144 propagation. It would be interesting to test these families in numerical implementations.
1145 In particular, various numerical studies in multiple contexts could provide useful tests of these BDNK families \cite{PandyaPretorius, PandyaMostPretorius, PandyaIdealGas, BantilanBeaFigueras, BeaFigueras, BhambureEtAl, FantiniRubio, ClarisseEtAl,  ShumEtAl}.
1146 
1147 \label{sec:nonlinear-discussion}
1148 Nonlinear causality and local existence and uniqueness in Gevrey classes
1149 have been established for several neutral constitutive
1150 classes~\cite{BDN2018,DisconziGevrey,BDN2019}. Applying these results
1151 requires matching the constitutive relations as well as checking the
1152 coefficient, regularity and initial-data hypotheses. For the thermodynamic
1153 neutral choices $\theta_1=\theta_2$, the coefficients used in
1154 Refs.~\cite{BDN2019,BDG} are related to ours by
1155 \[
1156  (\chi_1,\chi_2,\chi_3,\chi_4)
1157  =(x\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,x\pi_1,\pi_2),\qquad
1158  \lambda_{\mathrm{BDN}}=\theta_1=\theta_2.
1159 \]
1160 Under this identification, our causal bounds imply the algebraic
1161 conditions of Ref.~\cite{BDN2019}. Consequently, its results on local
1162 existence, uniqueness and nonlinear causality apply to the thermodynamic
1163 neutral members of our families, provided the constitutive functions
1164 and initial data satisfy the regularity and positivity assumptions
1165 stated there.
1166 
1167 Under the same coefficient identification, the neutral \family{6}
1168 subfamily with $\beta=0$, constant $1<\Lambda\leq2$ and $\eta>0$
1169 satisfies the algebraic conditions of Ref.~\cite{BDG}. Its result on
1170 local well-posedness in Sobolev spaces therefore applies in Minkowski
1171 spacetime, provided the EoS and constitutive functions satisfy the
1172 analyticity and positivity assumptions stated there, and the initial
1173 data satisfy the required Sobolev regularity and compatibility
1174 conditions.
1175 
1176 For charged fluids, Ref.~\cite{BDN2022} establishes nonlinear causality
1177 and local well-posedness in a constitutive class with $J^\mu=nu^\mu$.
1178 Our current contains additional derivative terms, so those results do
1179 not apply directly. Nonlinear causality conditions for generic charged frames, including
1180 non-thermodynamic frames, were derived in
1181 Ref.~\cite{AbboudSperanzaNoronha} from the characteristics of the full
1182 nonlinear equations. All six charged frame families constructed here
1183 satisfy these conditions for $\eta>0$ and $\mathcal D>0$, within their
1184 stated thermodynamic and parameter domains. Strong hyperbolicity has also been established for a restricted class
1185 of charged constitutive relations in Ref.~\cite{SchianchiAbalos}.
1186 Those results do not apply directly to the general families constructed
1187 here. Their result does apply to the exactly conformal family \family{6}
1188 with $\Lambda=\alpha_q=2$, provided the prescribed conductivity
1189 satisfies the additional restriction
1190 $\mathcal D=4\eta\Delta_J/(3w^2)$. Establishing
1191 well-posedness for the full families constructed here remains a further
1192 step beyond their linear stability and characteristic-speed bounds.
1193 
1194 A natural extension of the method developed here is magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) for which a BDNK approach has been developed in various works \cite{ArmasCamilloni, LierJainArmasPorth, HoultKovtunMHD, LierArmasPorth}. These developments motivate seeking coefficient families that keep
1195 anisotropic viscosities, resistivities and other physical transport
1196 prescribed. The magnetic field introduces direction dependent
1197 Alfv\'en and magnetosonic sectors and a magnetic constraint, so the
1198 neutral-to-charged map we established cannot simply be reused but the overall strategy of telegrapher factorisation of the longitudinal polynomial may work. The same strategy may also be useful for chiral fluids \cite{AbboudSperanzaNoronha} and for relativistic superfluids \cite{HoultShukla}. It would be interesting to apply the
1199 \href{https://jacomearmas.org/research/BDNK}{AI research protocol} developed through this work
1200 to these more complicated cases.

1244 \subsubsection{Neutral entropy production}\label{app:neutral-entropy}
1245 Here we re-derive neutral entropy production in a general frame in order to highlight the physical transport coefficients and their constraints. We write $T_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}$ for the first-derivative correction in
1246 \eqref{eq:stress}. The canonical neutral entropy current is
1247 \begin{equation}
1248  TS^\mu=p u^\mu-u_\nu T^{\mu\nu},\qquad
1249  S^\mu=su^\mu-\frac{u_\nu}{T}T_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}.
1250 \end{equation}
1251 The energy equation gives
1252 $D\epsilon+w\Theta=u_\nu\nabla_\mu T_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}$.
1253 Using $d\epsilon=T\,ds$ and $w=Ts$, one therefore obtains
1254 \begin{align}
1255  \nabla_\mu(su^\mu)&=
1256        \frac{u_\nu}{T}\nabla_\mu T_{(1)}^{\mu\nu},\\
1257  \nabla_\mu S^\mu&=-T_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}
1258                       \nabla_\mu\left(\frac{u_\nu}{T}\right).
1259  \label{app:eq:neutral-entropy-divergence}
1260 \end{align}
1261 Using the operators of Section~\ref{sec:constitutive}, direct contraction
1262 of \eqref{eq:stress} in \eqref{app:eq:neutral-entropy-divergence} yields
1263 \begin{align}
1264  T\nabla_\mu S^\mu={}&-\varepsilon_1(D\ln T)^2
1265  -(\varepsilon_2+\pi_1)(D\ln T)\Theta-\pi_2\Theta^2\nonumber\\
1266  &-\theta_1a_\mu a^\mu-(\theta_1+\theta_2)a_\mu\perpgrad^\mu\ln T
1267        -\theta_2(\perpgrad\ln T)^2\nonumber\\
1268  &+\frac\eta2\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+O(\partial^3).
1269 \end{align}
1270 The ideal energy and momentum equations imply
1271 \begin{equation}
1272  D\ln T=-x\Theta+O(\partial^2),\qquad

1338 \subsubsection*{Reducing the Routh-Hurwitz inequalities for every momentum}
1339 To reduce the inequalities, we use $R,\Sigma,\Phi,\Lh$ from \eqref{eq:invariants} and the Hurwitz
1340 determinants \eqref{eq:rh}. Their highest-order terms follow from the identity
1341 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-design-RH}
1342  A_LB_L^2\Lh=D_L(B_LC_L-A_LD_L)-B_L^2F_L.
1343 \end{equation}
1344 For example, expanding
1345 $D_Lq\{B_L(w^2+C_Lq)-A_LD_Lq\}-B_L^2(xw^2q+F_Lq^2)$
1346 and using $D_L-xB_L=w\Gamma$ gives $\Delta_3$ in \eqref{eq:rh}.
1347 With $a_0(q)=xw^2q+F_Lq^2$, the Routh array is
1348 \begin{equation}
1349  \begin{array}{c|ccc}
1350  \varsigma^4&A_L&w^2+C_Lq&a_0(q)\\
1351  \varsigma^3&B_L&D_Lq&0\\
1352  \varsigma^2&\Delta_2/B_L&a_0(q)&0\\
1353  \varsigma^1&\Delta_3/\Delta_2&0&0\\
1354  \varsigma^0&a_0(q)&0&0
1355  \end{array}.
1356 \end{equation}
1357 For each $q>0$, strict stability requires the first column to be positive.
1358 Under the stated positivity assumptions, imposing this for
1359 \emph{every} $q>0$ gives
1360 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-design-unreduced}
1361  F_L\geq0,\qquad B_LC_L-A_LD_L\geq0,\qquad \Lh\geq0.
1362 \end{equation}
1363 For example, a negative coefficient of the highest power of $q$ in
1364 $a_0$, $\Delta_2$, or $\Delta_3$ eventually violates the corresponding
1365 condition. Conversely, the positive lower-power terms ensure strict
1366 positivity at every finite $q>0$ even if a displayed inequality saturates.
1367 Because $D_L>0$, the first and third conditions imply the second.
1368 Thus the rest-frame sound stability test reduces to
1369 $\Phi,\Lh\geq0$ in \eqref{eq:certificate}.

1371 \subsubsection*{Adding the sound causality conditions}
1372 The sound characteristic polynomial is $A_Ly^2-C_Ly+F_L$.
1373 Its two roots are real and lie in $[0,1]$ if and only if
1374 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-design-causal}
1375  F_L\geq0,\quad C_L\geq0,\quad C_L^2\geq4A_LF_L,\quad
1376  C_L\leq2A_L,\quad A_L-C_L+F_L\geq0.
1377 \end{equation}
1378 Indeed, the first three conditions give real nonnegative roots.
1379 The last two give $y_-+y_+\leq2$ and
1380 $(1-y_-)(1-y_+)\geq0$, which force both roots to be at most one.
1381 This argument includes the boundary roots $0$ and $1$ without replacing a
1382 strict stability criterion by a non-strict one.
1383 Moreover,
1384 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-design-interlacing}
1385  A_LR^2-C_LR+F_L=-A_L\Lh\leq0.
1386 \end{equation}
1387 With $R>x>0$ and $F_L\geq0$, this already implies reality,
1388 nonnegativity, and $y_-\leq R\leq y_+$. Hence after solving the
1389 Routh-Hurwitz conditions, the remaining sound speed restrictions can be
1390 imposed as $C_L\leq2A_L$ and $A_L-C_L+F_L\geq0$.

1392 On the branch $\pi_2=4\eta/3$, one has $\Phi=0$ and the sound conditions
1393 reduce to $x<R\leq\Sigma\leq1$. Setting $\Sigma=1$ fixes
1394 $\theta_2$ through \eqref{eq:luminal-design} and gives
1395 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-design-principal-factor}
1396  \begin{aligned}
1397  P_{N,\rm pr}&=A_L\varsigma^2(\varsigma^2+q),\\
1398  P_N&=P_{N,\rm pr}+B_L\varsigma(\varsigma^2+Rq)+w^2(\varsigma^2+xq).
1399  \end{aligned}
1400 \end{equation}
1401 Only the principal part factorizes in this way. The remaining terms
1402 retain sound attenuation. Indeed, division by $\varsigma^2+xq$ leaves a
1403 nonzero $\varsigma q$ coefficient $D_L-xB_L=w\Gamma$ from
1404 \eqref{eq:quartic-data}, ruling out an undamped ideal sound factor when

1685 as in \cite{HoultKovtun}. On our regular domain
1686 it is positive definite and direct differentiation gives
1687 \[
1688  \begin{gathered}
1689  n_\mu=p_{\mu\mu}>0,\qquad \Delta_J=T^2\delta_p>0,\\
1690  E=T(p_{TT}+2\alpha p_{T\mu}+\alpha^2p_{\mu\mu})>0,\qquad
1691  wx=\frac{p_{\mu\mu}s^2-2p_{T\mu}sn+p_{TT}n^2}{\delta_p}.
1692  \end{gathered}
1693 \]
1694 Consequently
1695 \[
1696  wx n_\mu-n^2=\frac{(p_{\mu\mu}s-p_{T\mu}n)^2}{\delta_p}\geq0,
1697 \]
1698 which proves the last inequality in \eqref{eq:domain}. Using $x=A+rTB$ from \eqref{eq:thermo}, the other inequality
1699 follows from
1700 \[
1701  A+\frac{n_\mu}{w}(TB)^2-rTB
1702  =\frac{n_\mu}{w}\left(TB-\frac{n}{n_\mu}\right)^2
1703    +x-\frac{n^2}{wn_\mu}\geq0.
1704 \]

1706 restricts the ideal sound cone. We do not consider the case of singular susceptibilities nor vanishing/light speeds
1707 $x=0,1$.

1718 \subsubsection*{Charged entropy production and matching identities}
1719 \label{app:sec:charged-entropy}
1720 We begin by re-deriving the entropy constraints in an arbitrary frame stated in Section~\ref{sec:overview}. For the canonical current
1721 $S^\mu=(p u^\mu-u_\nu T^{\mu\nu}-\mu J^\mu)/T$, conservation and
1722 thermodynamics give
1723 \begin{equation}
1724  \nabla_\mu S^\mu=-T_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu(u_\nu/T)
1725                          -J_{(1)}^\mu\nabla_\mu\alpha,
1726 \end{equation}
1727 where $J_{(1)}^\mu$ is the first-derivative correction in \eqref{eq:current}.
1728 Using the scalar and vector corrections of \eqref{eq:scalars}--\eqref{eq:vectors},
1729 the contraction is
1730 \begin{equation}\label{app:charged-entropy-contraction}
1731  \begin{aligned}
1732  T\nabla_\mu S^\mu={}&-\mathcal E D\ln T-\mathcal P\Theta-T\mathcal N D\alpha\\
1733  &-\mathcal Q_\mu(a^\mu+\perpgrad^\mu\ln T)
1734  -T\mathcal J_\mu\perpgrad^\mu\alpha
1735  +\frac\eta2\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+O(\partial^3).
1736  \end{aligned}
1737 \end{equation}
1738 To reduce the entropy production using the ideal conservation equations,
1739 we first express $A$ and $B$ in terms of the susceptibilities
1740 \eqref{eq:suscept}. The pressure identities
1741 $(\partial_Tp)_\alpha=w/T$ and $(\partial_\alpha p)_T=Tn$, together
1742 with the Maxwell relation $F=T^2N$, give
1743 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:charged-uplift-thermo}
1744  A=\frac{wq_\alpha}{T{\Delta_J}},\qquad
1745  B=-\frac{wq_T}{{\Delta_J}}.
1746 \end{equation}
1747 These follow by expressing the derivatives in \eqref{eq:thermo} as
1748 Jacobians with respect to $(T,\alpha)$, whose transformation to
1749 $(\epsilon,n)$ has determinant $\Delta_J$.
1750 The ideal scalar equations
1751 $E D T+F D\alpha=-w\Theta$ and $N D T+H D\alpha=-n\Theta$
1752 give $D\ln T=-A\Theta$ and $D\alpha=-B\Theta$ on inversion.
1753 Ideal momentum conservation gives
1754 $a^\mu=-\perpgrad^\mu\ln T-rT\perpgrad^\mu\alpha$.
1755 
1756 Substituting the constitutive relations gives
1757 the following quadratic expression, before imposing any frame-coefficient
1758 relation to cancel a contribution:
1759 \begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
1760  \begin{aligned}
1761  \nabla_\mu S^\mu
1762  ={}&\frac{\eta}{2T}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}
1763  -\frac1T\Bigl[\eps_1(D\ln T)^2+(\eps_2+\pi_1)(D\ln T)\Theta+\pi_2\Theta^2\\
1764  &\quad +(\eps_3+T\nu_1)(D\ln T)(D\alpha)
1765           +(\pi_3+T\nu_2)\Theta D\alpha+T\nu_3(D\alpha)^2\\
1766  &\quad +\theta_1a_\mu a^\mu
1767           +(\theta_1+\theta_2)a_\mu\perpgrad^\mu\ln T
1768           +\theta_2(\perpgrad\ln T)^2\\
1769  &\quad +(\theta_3+T\gamma_1)a_\mu\perpgrad^\mu\alpha
1770           +(\theta_3+T\gamma_2)\perpgrad_\mu\ln T\,\perpgrad^\mu\alpha\\
1771  &\quad +T\gamma_3(\perpgrad\alpha)^2\Bigr]
1772           +\OO(\partial^3)\\
1773  ={}&\frac{\eta}{2T}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}
1774        +\frac\zeta T\Theta^2+\cD T(\perpgrad\alpha)^2\\
1775  &\quad +\bigl[r(\theta_2-\theta_1)-(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)\bigr]
1776               \perpgrad_\mu\ln T\,\perpgrad^\mu\alpha+\OO(\partial^3).
1777  \end{aligned}
1778 \end{equation}
1779 Only the last equality in \eqref{eq:entropy} uses these
1780 ideal equation relations, together with the definitions in
1781 \eqref{eq:matching}. The mixed term
1782 has not been set to zero. There is no remaining
1783 $(\perpgrad\ln T)^2$ term, so positivity for arbitrary independent
1784 $\perpgrad^\mu\ln T$ and $\perpgrad^\mu\alpha$ requires the vector
1785 relation in \eqref{eq:thermo-domain}. Imposing that equation makes
1786 the displayed quadratic production nonnegative. This condition permits unequal
1787 $\theta_1,\theta_2$ and unequal $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$. It does not impose
1788 a thermodynamic frame.

2096 \subsubsection{Boosted sound quartic: gaps, stability and causality}
2097 \label{app:sec:neutral-criterion}\label{sec:boosts}
2098 As explained in Section~\ref{sec:construction-strategy}, boosting a
2099 damped mode with a real wavevector in the fluid rest frame generally
2100 makes its transformed wavevector complex. To test stability in a moving
2101 frame, we instead prescribe a real wavevector $\boldsymbol k$ in that
2102 frame and solve for its complex growth rate $\varsigma$. Throughout this
2103 section, $k=|\boldsymbol k|$, and primes denote quantities in the fluid
2104 rest frame. We first check the gaps at $k=0$. For $k>0$, we then show that a root
2105 cannot acquire a positive real part as the boost varies: it can neither
2106 cross the imaginary axis nor enter from infinity. Finally, we check the
2107 characteristic speeds for every propagation angle. The common shear and charge
2108 proof follows in Section~\ref{app:sec:shear}, and the full charged system
2109 is treated in Section~\ref{app:sec:charged-boost-proof}.
2110 
2111 The characteristic roots are $y_\pm$ in \eqref{eq:speeds}, so
2112 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-principal}
2113  A_Ly^2-C_Ly+F_L=A_L(y-y_-)(y-y_+).
2114 \end{equation}
2115 \begin{proposition}\label{app:prop:neutral}
2116 Suppose $w>0$, $0<x<1$, $\Gamma>0$, $A_L>0$, $B_L>0$, and the
2117 sound inequalities in \eqref{eq:certificate} hold. These are its first
2118 three conditions, involving $\Phi$, $\Lh$ and $y_\pm$.
2119 Then every sound root at a real wavevector with $k>0$ satisfies $\Re \varsigma<0$
2120 for every $|v|<1$ and every propagation angle. At $k=0$ there are
2121 two roots at $\varsigma=0$ and two gapped modes with negative real parts. All sound
2122 characteristic velocities are real and have magnitude at most one.
2123 \end{proposition}

2125 Rest-frame stability follows from the earlier Routh--Hurwitz analysis
2126 in Section~\ref{app:sec:neutral-construction} and the gaps in
2127 Eq.~\eqref{eq:neutral-rest-gaps}. The assumptions $A_L,B_L>0$ imply
2128 $\varepsilon_1,\theta_1>0$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:quartic-data}. We now
2129 establish stability under arbitrary boosts, first checking the $k=0$
2130 gaps and then treating $k>0$.

2132 \subsubsection*{Boosted $k=0$ gaps}
2133 We align the boost with the $z$ axis. In the convention
2134 $\varsigma=-i\omega$, the Lorentz transformation gives
2135 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-boost}
2136  \varsigma'=\gamma(\varsigma+ivk_z),\qquad
2137  q'=k_\perp^2+\gamma^2(k_z-iv\varsigma)^2,\qquad
2138  \gamma=(1-v^2)^{-1/2}.
2139 \end{equation}
2140 The boosted polynomial is $P_{N,\mathrm{boost}}(\varsigma,k_z,k_\perp)=P_N(\varsigma',q')$.
2141 Here $k_\perp$ is the magnitude of the wavevector perpendicular to the
2142 boost. At $k=0$, we have $\varsigma'=\gamma\varsigma$ and
2143 $q'=-\gamma^2v^2\varsigma^2$. Substituting these into
2144 \eqref{eq:neutral-sound-polynomial} and removing the factor
2145 $\gamma^2\varsigma^2$ associated with the two gapless modes gives
2146 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-gaps}
2147  \frac{P_{N,\mathrm{boost}}(\varsigma,0,0)}{\gamma^2\varsigma^2}
2148  =\gamma^2(A_L-C_Lv^2+F_Lv^4)\varsigma^2
2149  +\gamma(B_L-D_Lv^2)\varsigma+w^2(1-xv^2).
2150 \end{equation}
2151 The leading coefficient is positive because
2152 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-leading}
2153  A_L-C_Lv^2+F_Lv^4
2154        =A_L(1-y_-v^2)(1-y_+v^2)>0\qquad (|v|<1).
2155 \end{equation}
2156 Equation~\eqref{app:eq:neutral-design-interlacing} gives
2157 $y_-\leq R\leq y_+\leq1$, so the linear coefficient is positive as well:
2158 $B_L-D_Lv^2=B_L(1-Rv^2)>0$. Finally, $0<x<1$ makes the constant term in
2159 \eqref{app:eq:neutral-gaps} positive. All three coefficients
2160 are therefore positive, proving that both gaps have negative real parts,
2161 including at luminal saturation $y_+=1$.

2163 \subsubsection*{Stability at every nonzero wavevector}
2164 We fix real $k_z,k_\perp$ with $k^2=k_z^2+k_\perp^2>0$.
2165 Either component may vanish, so this includes propagation parallel and
2166 perpendicular to the boost. Suppose that a root were on the imaginary
2167 axis of the growth-rate plane, $\varsigma=i\sigma$ with
2168 $\sigma\in\mathbb R$. Equation~\eqref{app:eq:neutral-boost} then gives
2169 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:boost-imaginary-root}
2170  \varsigma'=i\Omega,\quad \Omega=\gamma(\sigma+vk_z),\qquad
2171  K^2=q'=k_\perp^2+\gamma^2(k_z+v\sigma)^2\geq0.
2172 \end{equation}
2173 Thus $\Omega$ and $K$ are real. Substitution into
2174 \eqref{eq:neutral-sound-polynomial} gives
2175 \begin{align}
2176  \Im P_N(i\Omega,K^2)&=\Omega(-B_L\Omega^2+D_LK^2),\\
2177  \Re P_N(i\Omega,K^2)&=A_L\Omega^4-(w^2+C_LK^2)\Omega^2
2178                      +xw^2K^2+F_LK^4.
2179 \end{align}
2180 For a root, both parts must vanish. The imaginary part allows only
2181 $\Omega=0$ or $\Omega^2=RK^2$, since $R=D_L/B_L$.
2182 The possibility $K=\Omega=0$ is excluded: invertibility of the boost
2183 would give $\sigma=k_z=k_\perp=0$, contrary to $k>0$.
2184 If $\Omega=0$, we therefore have $K>0$ and
2185 $\Re P_N=xw^2K^2+F_LK^4>0$. If $\Omega^2=RK^2$, then
2186 \eqref{app:eq:neutral-design-interlacing} instead gives
2187 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-axis-exclusion}
2188  \Re P_N=w^2(x-R)K^2-A_L\Lh K^4<0.
2189 \end{equation}
2190 Here $R>x$ and $\Lh\geq0$ ensure the strict inequality. Neither case
2191 allows a root on the imaginary axis.
2192 
2193 It remains to exclude roots entering the right half-plane from infinity.
2194 For the fixed wavevector, we vary $v$ from zero to any chosen
2195 $v_*$ with $|v_*|<1$. Writing the boosted quartic as
2196 $\sum_{j=0}^4a_j(v)\varsigma^j$, its leading coefficient is
2197 \[
2198  a_4(v)=\gamma^4A_L(1-y_-v^2)(1-y_+v^2)>0.
2199 \]
2200 It has a positive lower bound on the closed interval between $0$ and
2201 $v_*$. The other coefficients are continuous and bounded there.
2202 The polynomial root bound
2203 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:boost-root-bound}
2204  |\varsigma|\leq1+\max_{0\leq j<4}|a_j(v)/a_4(v)|
2205 \end{equation}
2206 therefore bounds all roots uniformly along this interval. Since roots
2207 can neither cross the imaginary axis nor escape to infinity, all four
2208 remain in the left half-plane as $v$ varies from $0$ to $v_*$. The
2209 wavevector and final boost were arbitrary. Together with the $k=0$
2210 gap calculation, this proves stability for every real wavevector and
2211 every finite boost.

2214 \subsubsection*{Causality for all propagation angles}
2215 For either squared characteristic speed $y=y_\pm\in[0,1]$, the
2216 corresponding factor of the principal polynomial
2217 \eqref{app:eq:neutral-principal} gives
2218 $\omega'^2=y|\boldsymbol k'|^2$ in the fluid rest frame.
2219 To express this in the moving frame, we use the Lorentz transformations
2220 underlying \eqref{app:eq:neutral-boost},
2221 \[
2222  \omega'=\gamma(\omega-vk_z),\qquad
2223  k_z'=\gamma(k_z-v\omega),\qquad k_\perp'=k_\perp.
2224 \]
2225 Writing $k_z=k\cos\phi$, $k_\perp=k\sin\phi$ and $\omega=ck$,
2226 with $k>0$, and dividing the rest-frame equation by $\gamma^2k^2$ gives
2227 \[
2228  (c-v\cos\phi)^2
2229  =y\bigl[(\cos\phi-vc)^2+(1-v^2)\sin^2\phi\bigr].
2230 \]
2231 Expanding both sides gives the quadratic equation for the characteristic
2232 velocity $c$ in the moving frame,
2233 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-speed-quadratic}
2234  (1-yv^2)c^2-2v(1-y)\cos\phi\,c
2235  +v^2\cos^2\phi-y(1-v^2+v^2\cos^2\phi)=0.
2236 \end{equation}
2237 Its roots are
2238 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:neutral-speed-boosted}
2239  c_\pm(y,\phi)=\frac{v(1-y)\cos\phi
2240  \ \pm\sqrt{y(1-v^2)[1-v^2+v^2(1-y)\sin^2\phi]}}{1-yv^2}.
2241 \end{equation}
2242 The discriminant is nonnegative and $1-yv^2>0$. Moreover the characteristic
2243 covector obeys
2244 \[
2245  |\boldsymbol k|^2-\omega^2
2246  =|\boldsymbol k'|^2-\omega'^2=(1-y)|\boldsymbol k'|^2\geq0,
2247 \]
2248 so $|c_\pm|\leq1$. At $y=0$ the root is the advective speed $v\cos\phi$.
2249 At $y=1$ the roots are exactly $\pm1$. Applying this calculation to both
2250 $y_\pm$ completes the causality proof in Proposition~\ref{app:prop:neutral}.

2252 \subsubsection{Boosted shear and charge factors}
2253 \label{app:sec:shear}
2254 Both the shear and charge factors have the form
2255 \[
2256  Q(\varsigma,q)=a\varsigma^2+b\varsigma+dq,
2257  \qquad a\geq d>0,\quad b>0,
2258 \]
2259 where $a,b,d$ are constant coefficients at the equilibrium state.
2260 At rest, positivity gives two damped roots for $q>0$. After
2261 \eqref{app:eq:neutral-boost}, the leading coefficient is
2262 $\gamma^2(a-dv^2)>0$, and the $k=0$ roots are $0$ and
2263 $-b/[\gamma(a-dv^2)]<0$.
2264 For $k>0$, an imaginary-axis root would satisfy
2265 \[
2266  \Im Q(i\Omega,K^2)=b\Omega=0,\qquad
2267  \Re Q(0,K^2)=dK^2>0,
2268 \]
2269 where $\Omega,K$ are defined in \eqref{app:eq:boost-imaginary-root}.
2270 The second inequality follows from $K>0$ when $\Omega=0$ and $k>0$,
2271 and excludes such a root. The leading coefficient is bounded away
2272 from zero on each closed finite boost interval, so the root-bound
2273 argument of Section~\ref{app:sec:neutral-criterion} excludes roots
2274 entering from infinity. Both roots therefore remain damped at every
2275 nonzero real wavevector. Causality follows from
2276 \eqref{app:eq:neutral-speed-boosted} with $y=d/a\leq1$.
2277 
2278 For the shear sector, $(a,b,d)=(\theta_1,w,\eta)$, so the required
2279 bound is precisely \eqref{eq:strategy-shear-bound}. Each transverse
2280 polarization has the gap
2281 \begin{equation}\label{app:eq:shear-boosted-gap}
2282  \varsigma_{T,\mathrm{gap}}=-\frac{w}{\gamma(\theta_1-\eta v^2)}<0.
2283 \end{equation}
2284 No relation between $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ is used, and the proof
2285 includes the luminal endpoint $\theta_1=\eta$.\footnote{\label{fn:shear-zero-transport}At
2286 $\eta=0$, the shear polynomial becomes
2287 $\varsigma'(\theta_1\varsigma'+w)$. It has an undamped advected mode
2288 and, if $\theta_1>0$, a damped mode
2289 $\varsigma=-ivk_z-w/(\gamma\theta_1)$.
2290 If $\theta_1=0$, only advection remains. These modes are causal and
2291 linearly stable, but their comoving characteristic speed is zero.}
2292

2295 $\tau_q=\alpha_qD_q\geq D_q>0$. The same proof applies with
2296 $(a,b,d)=(\tau_q,1,D_q)$, establishing stability at all real
2297 wavevectors and the squared comoving characteristic speed
2298 $y_q=D_q/\tau_q\leq1$.
